Docebo Inc. · TSX: DCBO · Reverse DCF

The market is betting Docebo loses a quarter of its revenue every year

At C$33.22, Docebo's share price only makes sense if about 24.5% of its recurring revenue cancels each year. The company's reported 99% net revenue retention points to about 9%.

1. Company overview

Docebo sells a cloud learning platform that companies use to train employees, customers and partners. Revenue is almost entirely subscription-based (93% in Q2-2026), so annual recurring revenue (ARR) is the best measure of the business. The company is headquartered in Toronto, was founded in Italy, reports in US dollars, and is listed on the TSX and Nasdaq. Intercap owns about 64% of the shares.

Growth has slowed from about 18% in 2024 to 9.5% today. Two partner relationships are winding down: AWS (Amazon), Docebo's largest OEM customer at 6.2% of ARR in Sep-2025, and Dayforce. Management says underlying growth excluding these is about 14%, and that ARR growth re-accelerated in both Q1 and Q2 2026. Meanwhile profitability has improved: adjusted EBITDA margin was 16.4% in Q2-2026, with Q3 guidance implying about 23%.

2. Method: running the model backwards

A normal DCF picks assumptions and produces a share price. A reverse DCF starts from the share price and solves for the assumption the market must be using. We solve for churn because, for a subscription business, retention drives the value more than anything else. We work at the customer-dollar level instead of on a single growth rate.

① Ending ARR = Starting ARR + New ARR + Expansion − Churn ② Revenue = average(Starting ARR, Ending ARR) × 1.06 (services on top of ARR) ③ FCF = Revenue × EBITDA margin × FCF conversion ④ Value today = Σ FCF_t ÷ (1 + r)^t + [FCF_10 × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)] ÷ (1 + r)^10 ⑤ Price = (Enterprise value + cash − debt) ÷ shares × USD/CAD ⑥ Solve: Price(churn) = C$33.22 → churn = 24.5%

"Reported churn" isn't disclosed directly, so we derive it from net revenue retention (NRR): churn = expansion + (1 − NRR) = 8% + (1 − 99%) = 9%. All other inputs stay fixed at values anchored to reported numbers. Only churn moves.

3. The finding: four ways to read C$33.22

Solving for churn is our main lens. The same price can be read through any single lever, holding the others at base case. Each reading tells the same story: the market is assuming something far worse than the reported numbers.

Even with zero new customers ever, the model gives C$35.41 per share. That's above today's price.

In other words, the market is paying for Docebo's existing customer base only, and even then at a discount. It is pricing in no value at all for future sales.

5. Why the market might be right

Risks

  • AI disruption. Generative AI could make course building and delivery a commodity, which would push churn and prices the wrong way across the category.
  • Customer concentration. The AWS and Dayforce wind-downs show that large partner accounts can leave. Retention of 99% includes some of that drag already.
  • Hidden costs. Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock-based compensation, so our cash flows are somewhat generous.
  • Ownership structure. A 64% controlling shareholder and a small free float can keep a lasting discount on the shares.

What would prove the market wrong

  • Net revenue retention holding at 99% or above once AWS is fully out of the base.
  • Continued ARR re-acceleration (two straight quarters so far).
  • EBITDA margin expanding toward the ~23% implied by Q3 guidance.
  • Capital returns: management launched a US$70M share tender (substantial issuer bid) in July 2026.

6. Conclusion and what to watch

Docebo's share price is betting on a sharp deterioration. It needs roughly 24.5% of ARR to disappear every year, or an equally severe drop in margins or new sales. The company's reported figures show nothing close to that today. If retention holds near 99%, the stock looks materially undervalued on our assumptions. If AI disruption shows up in the retention numbers, the market will have been early rather than wrong.

Catalyst: Q3-2026 results (expected early November 2026). We'll re-run the model with the new ARR, retention and price, and log whether the market's implied churn rises or falls.
