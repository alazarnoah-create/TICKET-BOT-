Docebo Inc. · TSX: DCBO · Reverse DCF

The market is pricing Docebo as if its customers are quietly leaving

At C$33.22, Docebo's share price only makes sense if existing customers pay about 10% less every year: a net revenue retention of 90%. Docebo reports 99%.

1. Company overview

Docebo sells a cloud learning platform that companies use to train employees, customers and partners. Subscription revenue was 94% of total revenue in Q3-2025 and Q4-2025. The company is headquartered in Toronto, reports in US dollars, and is listed on the TSX and Nasdaq. Intercap owns 63.7% of the shares.

ARR growth slowed from 17.8% in Q3-2024 to 8.4% in Q4-2025, then recovered to 9.5% in Q2-2026. The 2026 figure includes ARR from 365Talents, a French company Docebo bought in January 2026. Its largest OEM customer fell from 9.4% of ARR in March 2025 to 2.5% in June 2026; management says the AWS churn is complete and Dayforce is winding down. Over the same period profitability improved: adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 18.5% over the last twelve months.

2. Method: reported numbers in, market expectation out

A normal DCF picks assumptions and produces a share price. A reverse DCF starts from the price and solves for the assumption the market must hold. We solve for net revenue retention because Docebo reports it and it drives the value of a subscription business more than any other number.

① Ending ARR = Starting ARR × NRR + New-customer ARR ② Revenue = average(Starting ARR, Ending ARR) × 1.061 (LTM revenue ÷ average ARR) ③ FCF = Revenue × adj. EBITDA margin × 87.7% (FY2025 FCF ÷ adj. EBITDA) ④ Value today = Σ FCF_t ÷ (1 + r)^t + [FCF_10 × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)] ÷ (1 + r)^10 ⑤ Price = (Enterprise value + cash − debt) ÷ shares × USD/CAD ⑥ Solve: Price(NRR) = C$33.22 → NRR = 90.3%

No analyst guesses. Every input is reported by Docebo, calculated from reported numbers, taken from management's own long-term target, or a stated market convention (10% discount rate, 2.5% terminal growth). New-customer ARR and the FCF conversion rate are held flat at their actual levels. The appendix lists each input and its source.

3. The finding: four ways to read C$33.22

Retention is the main lens, but the same price can be read through any single lever with the others held at reported levels. Each reading says the same thing: the market expects much worse than Docebo is reporting.

You don't even need management's margin target. At today's 18.5% margin, held forever, the model gives C$40.96, still 23% above the price.

The limit of the argument: if Docebo never signed another new customer, the model gives C$31.24, just below the price. So the market does price in some new sales, but only about a tenth of the current pace.

The model says the stock looks cheap if the reported numbers hold. These facts are reasons a sceptical investor might not trust them, or might demand a discount anyway.

The strangest one is the buyback. Management said the shares were undervalued and offered to buy up to US$70M worth, even raising the price to US$25.00. Shareholders kept almost all of their shares. That could mean holders think the stock is worth more than US$25, or that the float is so thin, with 63.7% held by one owner, that few sellers exist. Either way, the plan to borrow up to US$60M for it shows how willing the company is to add debt.

6. Conclusion and what to watch

On reported numbers, Docebo's price assumes a sharp deterioration: retention falling from 99% to about 90%, or new sales collapsing to a tenth of today's pace. The company's filings show neither yet. That points to undervaluation, but the red flags give real reasons for a discount: debt-funded deals, acquired growth, "adjusted" profit that excludes recurring severance, and a controlling shareholder.

Catalyst: Q3-2026 results (early November 2026). Watch three things: (1) any updated net revenue retention figure, now that AWS has fully churned; (2) organic ARR growth excluding 365Talents; (3) borrowings. We'll re-run the model and log whether implied retention moves toward or away from the reported figure.
