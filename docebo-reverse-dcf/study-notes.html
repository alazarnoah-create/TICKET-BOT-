The idea, like you've never seen finance before

Think of a rental house. Normally you'd say: "It earns $2,000 a month, so it's worth about $400,000." That's working forward.

Working backwards: "It's selling for $250,000. What rent must buyers expect?" If that's much lower than the real rent, buyers are scared of something, or the house is a bargain.

We do the same with a stock. The share price is the selling price. Retention (how much existing customers keep paying) is what we solve for.

Words to know (copy these into your notebook)

ARR
Annual recurring revenue: the yearly value of all active subscriptions right now.Docebo: US$255.1M on 30 Jun 2026 (Q2-2026 press release).
Net revenue retention (NRR)
Of every $100 existing customers paid last year, how much they pay this year, after cancellations, downgrades and upgrades.Docebo 2025: 99%, or 101% without AWS (Q4-2025 earnings call).
Net ARR lost
100% − NRR. How much existing customers shrink each year.Reported: 1%. Implied by the price: 9.8%.
ARR bridge
Starting ARR × NRR + new-customer ARR = ending ARR. Your SaaS model's roll-forward, in dollars.
Adjusted EBITDA
Profit before interest, taxes and depreciation, with some costs removed by the company (stock pay, severance, deal costs).Last 12 months: US$47.9M, 18.5% of revenue.
Free cash flow (FCF)
Cash from operations minus spending on equipment and software. The money actually left over.Last 12 months: US$48.7M.
Discount rate
How much you shrink future money each year, because $1 later is worth less than $1 now. At 10%, $110 next year = $100 today.
Terminal value
One lump for all the years after Year 10: FCF in Year 11 ÷ (discount rate − long-run growth).Here it's 53% of the total value.
Enterprise value (EV)
What the whole business is worth: the sum of all discounted cash flows.
Equity value
EV + cash − debt. What belongs to shareholders. Divide by shares to get value per share.
Reverse DCF
Fix the answer at today's price and solve for the assumption. That assumption is the market's hidden bet.
Goal Seek
Excel tool: "change this input until that output equals X." We use it to find the retention that gives C$33.22.
Sensitivity table
A grid of answers for many combinations of two inputs. Excel: Data → What-If Analysis → Data Table.

What the model assumes, and where each comes from

  • Retention 99%. Reported by Docebo for FY2025.
  • New-customer ARR of US$24.3M a year, held flat. Calculated: ARR grew 255.1 − 233.1 = 22.0, plus the 1% lost from existing customers (2.3).
  • Margin: 18.5% today, rising to 24% by 2028, then flat. 24% is management's own target.
  • 87.7% of EBITDA becomes free cash. Calculated from FY2025: 38.4 ÷ 43.8.
  • 10% discount rate and 2.5% growth forever. Standard conventions. A CAPM cross-check gives about 9%.

Red flags: what's sketchy about the company

  • Bought back shares far above today's price. US$100M at US$55 in 2023, US$47.1M at an average US$29.07 in 2025, US$60M at US$20.40 in March 2026. Today it's about US$23.74. (Buyback results 29 Dec 2023, 11 Mar 2026; FY2025 MD&A)
  • The second 2026 buyback flopped. Up to US$70M offered, even at a raised US$25.00, but only US$2.48M of shares were sold into it. (Releases of 17 Jul, 21 Aug, 11 Sep 2026)
  • Owners' equity went negative. US$74.1M at Dec 2025 fell to about −US$0.3M at Jun 2026, mostly because of the buybacks. (Q2-2026 financial statements)
  • Borrowing to do deals. Borrowings of US$88.0M against US$45.7M of cash at 30 Jun 2026. (Release of 17 Jul 2026)
  • Growth partly bought. 365Talents for US$61.3M in January 2026; headline ARR includes it. (Acquisition release 20 Jan 2026)
  • 2025 profit boosted by a one-off. US$37.5M net income included a US$13.1M tax recovery. 2026 so far: a US$1.6M loss in Q1, then US$2.3M profit in Q2. (FY2025 annual report; Q1/Q2-2026 releases)
  • "Adjusted" hides real costs. Severance of US$5.2M (FY2025) and US$6.2M (Q1-2026) is left out of adjusted EBITDA. (Financial statements)
  • New leadership almost everywhere. New CEO (2024), CFO, CRO, CMO and CTO (2025). (Docebo releases)
  • One owner in control. Intercap holds 63.7%. (Release of 11 Sep 2026)

The strongest counter-argument: if Docebo's long-run cash only matches the net profit analysts expect (13.5% of revenue in 2029), the model gives C$34.00, about the market price. (Excel: Sensitivity tab, margin stress test; analyst figures from Simply Wall St)

One-page write-up (draft, ready to edit)

What Docebo's stock price is betting on

The company. Docebo (TSX: DCBO) sells a learning platform by subscription. It has US$255.1M of annual recurring revenue, 79.4% gross margins and US$48.7M of free cash flow over the last twelve months. The stock trades at C$33.22, about 2.5× ARR and 13× free cash flow.

The question. I built a 10-year ARR model from Docebo's reported numbers, turned it into free cash flow using its actual cash conversion and management's own margin target, discounted it at 10%, and solved for the net revenue retention that makes the model equal the market price.

The finding. The price implies retention of about 90%, meaning existing customers spend about 10% less each year. Docebo reports 99%. At reported numbers the model values the shares at about C$53.60, but only about C$34 if you value it on the net profit analysts expect.

The catch. On the net profit analysts expect, the price is about fair (C$34). And there are real red flags: buybacks at far higher prices, negative equity, debt-funded deals, a one-off tax gain in 2025 profit, and a new leadership team.

Conclusion. Either the reported retention won't hold, or the market is too pessimistic. The next results will show which, especially retention now that AWS has fully churned.

Illustrative student analysis, not investment advice. All figures from Docebo's public disclosures.

Interview answers to memorize

Your 30-second pitch

"I built a reverse DCF on Docebo using only its reported numbers. Instead of forecasting a price, I solved for the retention rate the market price implies. At C$33, the market is pricing in customers spending about 10% less each year, while Docebo reports 99% retention. I also found some red flags: a buyback nobody sold into, debt-funded deals and a controlling shareholder. So the stock looks cheap on paper, but there are reasons for a discount. Retention in the next results will show who's right."

"Walk me through a DCF."
Forecast free cash flow for about 10 years. Discount each year back to today. Add a terminal value for the years after that, also discounted. That gives enterprise value. Add cash and subtract debt for equity value, then divide by shares.
"Where do your numbers come from?"
Every input is reported by Docebo, calculated from reported figures, or management's own target. The only conventions are the 10% discount rate and 2.5% terminal growth, and I show a sensitivity grid for those.
"What's the weakest part?"
New-customer ARR includes an acquisition Docebo doesn't break out, and adjusted EBITDA excludes recurring severance. The terminal value is also over half the value.
